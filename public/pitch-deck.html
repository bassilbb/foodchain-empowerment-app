01 · The Opportunity

Nigeria buys food every day.
It just has no digital home.

Over 200 million people depend on open markets, kiosks and neighbourhood stores. Commerce is huge, but discovery, trust and pricing remain offline and informal.

◆

Fragmented supply

Food is sold through millions of disconnected micro-vendors with no shared catalogue or reach beyond their street.

◆

Price opacity

The same bag of rice costs wildly different amounts in Lagos, Kano and Enugu — and nobody knows until they arrive.

◆

Broken logistics

Buyers travel to markets; vendors struggle to deliver. There is no reliable, transparent fulfilment layer.

Informal, essential, and still almost entirely offline.
02 · The Problem

One country. Very different price tags.

Transport, storage and local demand mean the true cost of food changes as you cross a state line. Most platforms ignore this — and quietly lose money or overcharge buyers.

Food reaches communities unevenly — so costs and prices differ by state.
  • Buyers can't trust that the price they see is the price near them.
  • Vendors lose sales because they can't price for each market they serve.
  • Platforms that use one flat national price either bleed margin or price themselves out.

The insight

Price is local. A credible food marketplace must price every product, for every state, separately — and lock that price into the order the moment it is placed.

06 · The Engine

The state-pricing engine.

Each product holds an explicit selling price for every one of Nigeria's 37 states. Buyers always see their true local price — and orders store that price forever.

Local supply, local costs, local prices.
  • Explicit price rows — product × state = one authoritative price, editable by admins.
  • Live recalculation — cards, product pages, cart and checkout all read the selected state.
  • Order snapshots — the pricing state is frozen into every order, so history never changes.
  • 37 canonical states — a single source of truth, defaulting to FCT Abuja.

Why it matters

This is the difference between a demo catalogue and a marketplace that can actually operate profitably across Nigeria.
